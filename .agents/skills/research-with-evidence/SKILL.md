# Research With Evidence Skill

## Purpose
Establishes a rigorous methodology for gathering, evaluating, and documenting evidence for pedagogical, scientific, and architectural questions.

## Source Hierarchy
1. **Level 1**: Meta-analyses and systematic reviews in peer-reviewed scientific journals.
2. **Level 2**: Primary empirical research studies and randomized controlled trials.
3. **Level 3**: Authoritative academic textbooks and institutional standards (e.g., Cambridge, CEFR, Council of Europe).
4. **Level 4**: Established practitioner guidelines and authoritative engineering documentation.
5. **Level 5**: Anecdotal reports, forum discussions, and unsupported blog posts (treated strictly as unproven hypotheses).

## Workflow

### 1. Formulate Core Question
- Frame the question with precision (e.g., *"What optimal retention target for FSRS balances daily review time against long-term forgetting in A1 language learners?"*).

### 2. Evidence Gathering
- Query academic, official, and empirical sources.
- Collect citations, methodologies, sample sizes, and quantitative findings.

### 3. Analyze Conflicting Evidence
- Actively search for counter-evidence or boundary conditions where the primary finding breaks down.

### 4. Synthesize Conclusion & Confidence Level
- Assign a confidence rating:
  - **High**: Multiple independent meta-analyses or established scientific consensus.
  - **Moderate**: Several peer-reviewed studies with consistent outcomes.
  - **Low / Preliminary**: Single study or conflicting findings requiring further investigation.
  - **Hypothesis**: Plausible theoretical claim lacking direct empirical validation.

### 5. Document Findings
- Record synthesis in `docs/research/` linking primary sources and explicit takeaways for product design.
