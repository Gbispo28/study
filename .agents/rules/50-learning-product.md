# Learning Product & Science Rules

## 1. Scientific Evidence Over Opinion
- All pedagogical methodologies, learning routines, and scheduling mechanisms must be justified by scientific literature.
- Hierarchy of sources:
  1. Primary research in peer-reviewed SLA / cognitive science journals.
  2. Systematic reviews and meta-analyses.
  3. University and institutional publications.
  4. Reputable domain practitioner guidelines.
- Always distinguish between established consensus, emerging evidence, working hypothesis, and subjective opinion.

## 2. Core Cognitive & SLA Pillars
- **Active Recall & Retrieval Practice**: Information must be actively retrieved from memory, not passively reread.
- **Spaced Repetition (SRS/FSRS)**: Memory traces decay exponentially; intervals between reviews must expand dynamically according to stability and difficulty.
- **Comprehensible Input ($i+1$)**: Input material must be mostly understandable ($i$), with a manageable margin of new linguistic challenge ($+1$).
- **Deliberate Practice & Immediate Feedback**: Practice must be effortful, targeted at current weaknesses, and accompanied by corrective feedback.
- **Interleaving**: Mix distinct language skills, topics, and structures rather than massed single-topic cramming.

## 3. Holistic Linguistic Competencies
- The learning architecture must support all six essential pillars:
  1. **Listening** (phoneme discrimination, parsing natural speech)
  2. **Speaking** (oral production, conversational agility)
  3. **Reading** (comprehension, vocabulary in context)
  4. **Writing** (syntax, functional composition)
  5. **Pronunciation** (articulatory mechanics, prosody, stress)
  6. **Grammar & Vocabulary** (functional patterns, high-frequency lexemes)
- Align target proficiencies with the **CEFR** framework (A1 to C2).
