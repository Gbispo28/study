# CEFR Qualitative Assessment Rubrics: English Learning OS

> **Source Framework**: Grounded in the *Common European Framework of Reference for Languages: Learning, Teaching, Assessment – Companion Volume* (Council of Europe, 2020).
> **Scope**: Qualitative rubrics spanning the full spectrum: **Pre-A1, A1, A2, B1, B2, C1, and C2**.
> **Regulatory & Measurement Scope Disclaimer**: This rubric is designed for internal diagnostic profiling and curriculum adaptation within the English Learning OS. It does **NOT constitute an official or accredited Council of Europe certification examination**. Furthermore, the internal custom diagnostic battery operates with an explicit measurement ceiling at **B2**; descriptors for C1 and C2 serve as target proficiency references and require extended or external certified examination.

## 2. Dimension-Specific Qualitative Rubrics (Pre-A1 to C2)

### 2.1 Acoustic Listening Comprehension

| CEFR Level | Qualitative Descriptors (CEFR 2020) | Behavioral Diagnostic Criteria in English Learning OS |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Pre-A1** | Can recognize familiar everyday words, basic numbers, and simple greetings when spoken very slowly and clearly with long pauses. | Can identify isolated numbers, days, or words like *yes, no, sorry, name*. Fails on full sentences. |
| **A1** | Can understand everyday expressions aimed at the satisfaction of simple concrete needs, provided speech is very slow and distinct. | Understands short, simple questions (*"Where do you live?"*) at $<0.75\text{x}$ speed. Misses connected speech reductions (*gonna, wanna*). |
| **A2** | Can understand phrases and expressions related to areas of immediate priority, provided speech is clearly articulated. | Grasps main points in short workplace announcements. Tolerates moderate connected speech; lost if native speech accelerates. |
| **B1** | Can understand the main points of clear standard speech on familiar matters regularly encountered in work, school, and leisure. | Follows tech standups, tutorials, and straightforward explanations in standard accents. |
| **B2** | Can understand extended speech and lectures and follow complex lines of argument provided the topic is reasonably familiar. | Comfortably parses fast native podcasts and technical discussions with natural connected speech. |
| **C1** | Can understand enough to follow extended speech on abstract and complex topics beyond their own field, recognizing a wide range of idiomatic expressions. | Follows high-speed multi-speaker debates, rapid colloquial cross-talk, and subtle technical trade-off nuances. |
| **C2** | Has no difficulty understanding any kind of spoken language, whether live or broadcast, even when delivered at fast native speed. | Fully parses heavily accented, fast, and informal English across all cultural registers effortlessly. |

### 2.2 Reading Comprehension

| CEFR Level | Qualitative Descriptors (CEFR 2020) | Behavioral Diagnostic Criteria in English Learning OS |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Pre-A1** | Can recognize familiar names, words, and very basic phrases on simple signs. | Recognizes UI labels (*Save, Cancel, Delete*). Cannot parse a 2-sentence narrative. |
| **A1** | Can understand very short, simple texts, a single phrase at a time, picking up familiar names, words, and basic phrases. | Extracts facts from simple forms, time schedules, and short 20-word messages with high lexical repetition. |
| **A2** | Can understand short, simple texts containing high-frequency vocabulary, including international technical cognates. | Reads simplified graded readers (Level 1–2, 600 headwords) and basic documentation setup steps. |
| **B1** | Can read straightforward, factual texts on subjects related to their field of interest with satisfactory comprehension. | Reads technical blog posts, GitHub issue threads, and StackOverflow answers with occasional lookups. |
| **B2** | Can read with a large degree of independence, adapting style and speed of reading to different texts and purposes. | Rapidly skims official API documentation, complex whitepapers, and general news articles without fatigue. |
| **C1** | Can understand in detail long, complex texts, whether or not they relate to their area of speciality. | Comprehends dense legal licenses, abstract academic papers, and nuanced stylistic prose. |
| **C2** | Can understand and interpret critically virtually all forms of written language including structurally or linguistically complex texts. | Effortlessly reads complex philosophical or historical treatises, archaic prose, and highly technical research. |

### 2.3 Spoken Production & Interaction

| CEFR Level | Qualitative Descriptors (CEFR 2020) | Behavioral Diagnostic Criteria in English Learning OS |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Pre-A1** | Can produce simple isolated words and basic formulaic phrases. | Single-word fragments (*"Yes... programmer... computer"*). Latency $>5$ seconds. No connected syntax. |
| **A1** | Can produce simple, mainly isolated phrases. Can interact in a simple way with slow repetition and repair. | Simple SVO sentences (*"I work in company. I use Python."*). Heavy reliance on memorized frames. |
| **A2** | Can describe daily routines, workplace tasks, and personal background in short series of simple phrases and sentences. | 4–5 linked sentences (*"I start at nine. Then I check logs because query was slow."*). Participates in structured AI dialogue. |
| **B1** | Can enter unprepared into conversations on familiar topics and give brief explanations for opinions and plans. | Spontaneously participates in daily standups; explains why a bug occurred and what solutions were attempted. |
| **B2** | Can interact with a degree of fluency and spontaneity that makes regular interaction with native speakers quite possible without strain. | Defends an architectural trade-off in a live technical review; negotiates requirements effectively. |
| **C1** | Can express themselves fluently and spontaneously, almost effortlessly, selecting suitable phrases from a wide repertoire. | Leads international technical negotiations; explains complex distributed systems under high communicative pressure. |
| **C2** | Can convey finer shades of meaning precisely using a wide range of modification devices, showing great flexibility. | Speaks with complete communicative freedom, humor, idiomatic precision, and rhetorical mastery. |

### 2.4 Phonological Control (2020 Intelligibility Norms)

| CEFR Level | Council of Europe (2020) Official Descriptor | Behavioral Indicators & BP Contrastive Patterns |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Pre-A1** | Pronunciation of a very limited repertoire can be understood with difficulty by sympathetic listeners. | Pervasive epenthesis (*stop-i, dog-ue*); vowel mergers (/i/-/ɪ/, /æ/-/ɛ/); native syllable-timed cadence. |
| **A1** | Pronunciation can be understood with difficulty by speakers used to dealing with non-native speakers. | Epenthesis frequent on final stops; word stress errors frequently impede comprehension. |
| **A2** | Pronunciation is generally clear enough to be understood. A foreign accent is evident, but rarely impedes communication. | Epenthesis suppressed on high-frequency lexis; vowel contrasts inconsistent but context resolves meaning. |
| **B1** | Pronunciation is clearly intelligible even if an accent is obvious. Prosody and word stress are controlled on familiar lexis. | High intelligibility; tonic word stress consistently correct on general and technical lexis; unstressed vowels reduced to schwa. |
| **B2** | Has acquired a natural pronunciation and intonation which can be easily understood by all listeners. | Fluid connected speech; natural flapping, linking, and stress-timing; comfortably intelligible internationally. |
| **C1** | Can articulate fluently with effective phonological control of prosody, sentence stress, and fine shades of emphasis. | Native-like articulatory control; effortless rhythm and intonation across varied communicative registers. |
| **C2** | Can employ the full range of phonological features with complete naturalness and precision to convey subtle emphasis. | Complete articulatory mastery across formal, informal, and colloquial phonetic environments. |

### 2.5 Written Composition & Synthesis

| CEFR Level | Qualitative Descriptors (CEFR 2020) | Behavioral Diagnostic Criteria in English Learning OS |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Pre-A1** | Can write simple isolated words and numbers, basic personal details. | Writes name, email, job title. Cannot formulate a complete sentence without a model. |
| **A1** | Can write simple isolated phrases and sentences linked with basic connectors (*and, but*). | 2–3 short sentences (*"I am software engineer. I like coding."*). Frequent spelling/agreement errors. |
| **A2** | Can write a series of simple phrases and sentences linked with simple connectors (*and, but, because, then, so*). | Coherent 50-word workplace messages and simple bug reports using past simple. |
| **B1** | Can write straightforward connected texts on familiar subjects by linking shorter elements into a linear sequence. | Structured pull request descriptions, documentation summaries, and incident updates with clear paragraphs. |
| **B2** | Can write clear, detailed texts synthesizing information and evaluating arguments. | Authors complete architectural decision records, technical specifications, and detailed analytical reports. |
| **C1** | Can write clear, well-structured expositions of complex subjects, underlining relevant salient issues. | Compiles executive whitepapers, complex technical standards, and persuasive domain proposals. |
| **C2** | Can write clear, smoothly flowing, complex texts in an appropriate style and with an effective logical structure. | Authors authoritative domain treatises, published academic literature, and nuanced critique. |

---

## 3. Rubric Evaluation Rules

1. **Predominance Rule**: Assigned a CEFR band if performance meets at least 80% of descriptor criteria for that band.
2. **Boundary Bands**: Assigned when performance straddles levels (e.g., `A2-high / B1-low`).
3. **Decoupled Evaluation**: Receptive and productive dimensions are scored independently. A learner may be B2 in Reading while Pre-A1 in Spoken Production.
