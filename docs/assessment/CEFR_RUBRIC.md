# CEFR Qualitative Assessment Rubrics: English Learning OS

> **Source**: Grounded in the *Common European Framework of Reference for Languages: Learning, Teaching, Assessment – Companion Volume* (Council of Europe, 2020).
> **Scope**: Standardized qualitative descriptors mapping levels **Pre-A1, A1, A2, B1, and B2** across the diagnostic dimensions of the English Learning OS.

---

## 1. Official CEFR Communicative Modes vs Diagnostic Dimensions

| English Learning OS Dimension | Primary Official CEFR Companion Volume (2020) Scale |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Acoustic Listening** | Overall Listening Comprehension; Understanding Conversation; Listening to Announcements |
| **Text Reading** | Overall Reading Comprehension; Reading for Information; Reading as a Leisure Activity |
| **Spoken Production** | Overall Spoken Production; Sustained Monologue (Describing Experience / Putting a Case) |
| **Spoken Interaction** | Overall Spoken Interaction; Conversation; Goal-Oriented Cooperation |
| **Written Production** | Overall Written Production; Creative & Functional Writing |
| **Phonological Control** | **Phonological Control** (Sound Articulation, Prosody, Intelligibility) |
| **General Linguistic Range** | General Linguistic Range; Vocabulary Range; Grammatical Accuracy |

## 2. Dimension-Specific Qualitative Rubrics

### 2.1 Acoustic Listening Comprehension

| CEFR Level | Qualitative Descriptors (CEFR 2020) | Behavioral Diagnostic Criteria in English Learning OS |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Pre-A1** | Can recognize familiar everyday words, basic numbers, and simple greetings when spoken very slowly and clearly with long pauses. | Can identify isolated numbers, days of the week, or words like *yes, no, sorry, name*. Fails on any full sentence. |
| **A1** | Can understand everyday expressions aimed at the satisfaction of simple concrete needs, provided people articulate very slowly and distinctly. | Understands short, simple questions (*"Where do you live?"*) when spoken at $<0.75\text{x}$ speed. Completely misses connected speech reductions (*gonna, wanna*). |
| **A2** | Can understand phrases and expressions related to areas of immediate priority (e.g., basic personal/family info, local geography, employment, routine tasks), provided speech is clearly articulated. | Grasps the main point in short, clear workplace announcements. Tolerates moderate connected speech, but misses meaning if tempo accelerates. |
| **B1** | Can understand the main points of clear standard speech on familiar matters regularly encountered in work, school, leisure, including short narratives. | Follows tech standup updates, YouTube tutorials, and technical explanations if delivery is relatively clear and in standard accent. |
| **B2** | Can understand extended speech and lectures and follow complex lines of argument provided the topic is reasonably familiar. Can understand most TV news and current affairs programs. | Comfortably parses fast native speech, podcasts, and heated team meetings with varied accents and natural colloquialisms. |

---

### 2.2 Reading Comprehension

| CEFR Level | Qualitative Descriptors (CEFR 2020) | Behavioral Diagnostic Criteria in English Learning OS |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Pre-A1** | Can recognize familiar names, words, and very basic phrases on simple signs or menus. | Can identify UI button labels (*Save, Cancel, Delete, Submit*). Cannot comprehend a 2-sentence descriptive text. |
| **A1** | Can understand very short, simple texts, a single phrase at a time, picking up familiar names, words, and basic phrases and rereading as required. | Can extract facts from simple forms, time schedules, and short 20-word messages with high lexical repetition. |
| **A2** | Can understand short, simple texts containing the highest-frequency vocabulary, including a proportion of shared international words. | Reads simplified graded readers (Level 1–2, 600 headwords), basic documentation setup steps, and short operational Slack updates. |
| **B1** | Can read straightforward, factual texts on subjects related to their field of interest with a satisfactory level of comprehension. | Reads technical blog posts, GitHub issue threads, and StackOverflow answers with occasional dictionary lookups. |
| **B2** | Can read with a large degree of independence, adapting style and speed of reading to different texts and purposes. Has a broad active reading vocabulary. | Rapidly skims official API documentation, complex whitepapers, and general news articles without significant fatigue. |

### 2.3 Spoken Production & Interaction

| CEFR Level | Qualitative Descriptors (CEFR 2020) | Behavioral Diagnostic Criteria in English Learning OS |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Pre-A1** | Can produce simple isolated words and basic formulaic phrases about people and places. Can ask and answer simple questions about personal details using memorized patterns. | Utters single-word fragments (*"Yes... programmer... computer"*). High hesitation (>5 seconds). Cannot sustain turn-taking. |
| **A1** | Can produce simple, mainly isolated phrases about people and places. Can interact in a simple way, but communication is dependent on repetition at a slower rate of speech, rephrasing, and repair. | Produces simple SVO sentences (*"I work in company. I use Python."*). Heavy reliance on memorized frames. High latency in retrieval. |
| **A2** | Can give a simple description or presentation of people, living or working conditions, daily routines, likes/dislikes in short series of simple phrases and sentences. Can communicate in simple and routine tasks requiring a simple and direct exchange of information. | Describes daily routine in 4–5 linked sentences (*"I start at nine. Then I check logs, because query was slow."*). Can participate in structured AI dialogue. |
| **B1** | Can reasonably describe events, experiences, dreams, hopes, and ambitions, and briefly give reasons and explanations for opinions and plans. Can enter unprepared into conversations on familiar topics. | Participates spontaneously in daily standups; explains why a technical bug occurred, what solutions were attempted, and outlines next steps with manageable pauses. |
| **B2** | Can give clear, systematically developed presentations, with highlighting of significant points, and can account for and sustain opinions in discussion. Can interact with a degree of fluency and spontaneity that makes regular interaction with native speakers quite possible without imposing strain on either party. | Defends an architectural trade-off in a live technical review; negotiates requirements and provides constructive technical pushback. |

### 2.4 Phonological Control (2020 De-emphasized Native Norms)

*Note: In accordance with the Council of Europe 2020 Companion Volume, phonological control is graded on **Intelligibility**, **Articulatory Mechanics**, and **Prosody**, NOT on accent erasure.*

| CEFR Level | Council of Europe (2020) Official Descriptor | Specific BP Interference Behavioral Indicators |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Pre-A1** | Pronunciation of a very limited repertoire of learnt words and phrases can be understood with difficulty by sympathetic listeners. | Pervasive epenthesis (*stop-i, dog-ue, internet-y*); vowel mergers (/i/-/ɪ/, /æ/-/ɛ/); native syllable-timed Portuguese cadence. |
| **A1** | Pronunciation of a very limited repertoire of learnt words and phrases can be understood with difficulty by speakers used to dealing with non-native speakers. | Epenthesis remains frequent on final consonant clusters; word stress errors often cause listener misunderstanding. |
| **A2** | Pronunciation is generally clear enough to be understood, but conversational partners will need to ask for repetition from time to time. A strong foreign accent is evident, and mispronunciations occur, but rarely impede communication. | Epenthesis suppressed on common words; vowel contrasts (/i/ vs /ɪ/) inconsistent but context resolves meaning; basic stress-timing emerging. |
| **B1** | Pronunciation is clearly intelligible even if a foreign accent is obvious and occasional mispronunciations occur. Prosody and word stress are generally controlled on familiar lexis. | High intelligibility; tonic word stress consistently correct on general and technical vocabulary; unstressed vowels reduced to schwa /ə/. |
| **B2** | Has acquired a natural pronunciation and intonation which can be easily understood by all listeners. Can vary intonation and place sentence stress correctly to express fine shades of meaning. | Fluid GAE connected speech; natural flapping, linking, and rhythm; comfortably intelligible in international multi-accent environments. |

### 2.5 Written Composition & Sentence Combining

| CEFR Level | Qualitative Descriptors (CEFR 2020) | Behavioral Diagnostic Criteria in English Learning OS |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Pre-A1** | Can write simple isolated words and numbers, basic personal details, and familiar names. | Can write name, job title, email address. Cannot formulate a complete written sentence without model text. |
| **A1** | Can write simple isolated phrases and sentences linked with basic connectors like *and, but, because*. | Writes 2–3 short sentences (*"I am software engineer. I work from home and I like coding."*). Frequent spelling and agreement errors. |
| **A2** | Can write a series of simple phrases and sentences linked with simple connectors like *and, but, because, then, so*. | Writes coherent 50-word workplace messages and simple bug reports using past simple and basic causal connectors. |
| **B1** | Can write straightforward connected texts on a range of familiar subjects within his/her field of interest, by linking a series of shorter discrete elements into a linear sequence. | Writes structured pull request descriptions, documentation summaries, and customer-facing incident updates with clear paragraphing. |
| **B2** | Can write clear, detailed texts on a variety of subjects related to his/her field of interest, synthesizing and evaluating information and arguments. | Authors complete architectural decision records, technical specifications, and detailed analytical reports with varied syntactic complexity. |

---

## 3. Rubric Application Rules

1. **Predominance Rule**: A learner is assigned a CEFR band for a specific dimension if their performance meets at least 80% of the descriptor criteria for that band.
2. **The Plus Bands (+)**: When performance consistently satisfies one level and partially demonstrates the lower threshold of the next level, assign a plus band (e.g., `A2+` or `A2-high / B1-low`).
3. **Decoupled Evaluation**: Under no circumstances may a high reading score (e.g., B1) inflate a low spoken production score (e.g., Pre-A1). Each dimension must be evaluated independently.
