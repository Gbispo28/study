# Evidence Traceability Matrix: English Learning OS

> **Purpose**: This matrix serves as the single source of empirical truth for all pedagogical, psychological, and algorithmic decisions within the **English Learning OS**.
> Every material claim is classified under the project's canonical 8-tier epistemic hierarchy:
> 1. `ESTABLISHED`: Supported by robust, replicated meta-analyses, systematic reviews, or foundational cognitive consensus.
> 2. `STRONG EVIDENCE`: Supported by multiple peer-reviewed empirical studies / randomized trials with consistent outcomes.
> 3. `MODERATE EVIDENCE`: Supported by well-designed primary studies, but with boundary conditions or moderate sample sizes.
> 4. `LIMITED EVIDENCE`: Small-scale trials, preliminary laboratory data, or exploratory findings.
> 5. `EXPERT CONSENSUS`: Guidelines from authoritative international bodies (e.g., Council of Europe, Cambridge) or leading domain scholars without direct randomized trial evidence.
> 6. `PLAUSIBLE / HYPOTHESIS`: Conceptually sound theoretical model or hypothesis lacking direct empirical validation.
> 7. `UNSUPPORTED`: Claim lacking empirical evidence, based on myth, or contradicted by modern cognitive SLA research.
> 8. `CONFLICTING EVIDENCE`: Research literature shows divergent or contradictory results under different experimental conditions.

## 2. Epistemic Audit of Initial Repository Hypotheses

| Hypothesis ID | Original Formulation | Audit Verdict | Empirical Findings & Rationale | Revised Operational Form |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **HYP-01** | *Contextual sentence cards produce 2x higher retention than isolated word translation cards.* | **REVISED (Claim of 2x unsupported)** | Peer-reviewed vocabulary studies (Webb, 2007; Waring, 2004) demonstrate that while sentence context aids collocations and syntactical understanding, isolated word cards actually allow faster initial form-meaning acquisition. No empirical study supports a literal "2x retention rate". Sentence cards take 2–3x longer to review. | **REQ-HYP-01 (Revised)**: Use lean/bilingual flashcards for initial form-meaning acquisition of the top 500 concrete lemmas; transition to contextual sentence/cloze cards for polysemous words, prepositions, collocations, and grammatical patterns to foster collocational depth. |
| **HYP-02** | *FSRS with an 85% retention target will minimize daily Anki review load compared to default Anki SM-2.* | **REVISED (Target parameter unvalidated)** | FSRS documentation and simulator data (Ye, 2023) confirm FSRS reduces review load by ~20–30% compared to SM-2 at equivalent retention. However, 85% desired retention increases card lapses and relearning overhead compared to the official default (90%). For beginners acquiring high-frequency core vocabulary, a lapse penalty creates cognitive friction. | **REQ-HYP-02 (Revised)**: Adopt FSRS scheduling. Set initial desired retention to **90%** for the core foundational vocabulary (first 1,000 words) to guarantee high recognition stability. Dynamically evaluate downscaling to 85–87% only after the learner accumulates $>500$ mature cards and reviews exceed 20 min/day. |
| **HYP-03** | *Prioritizing phonemic discrimination in weeks 1–4 will prevent fossilized pronunciation errors common among Portuguese speakers.* | **REVISED (Absolute prevention unsupported)** | High-Variability Phonetic Training (HVPT) significantly improves adult phonemic discrimination and production (Logan et al., 1991; Bradlow et al., 1997). However, second language acquisition literature (Han, 2004) confirms that "fossilization" cannot be completely or permanently prevented by a 4-week initial drill block; phonological interlanguage remains dynamic. | **REQ-HYP-03 (Revised)**: Implement dedicated contrastive phonological training targeting Brazilian Portuguese interference (vowel contrasts, final epenthesis, rhythm) starting in Week 1, maintaining micro-drills integrated into daily listening/speaking to maximize intelligibility and functional comprehensibility. |
| **HYP-04** | *Controlled AI dialogue drills with real-time speech-to-text and grammar feedback will reduce anxiety compared to open-ended conversational apps.* | **SUPPORTED (With pedagogical boundaries)** | Empirical research on computer-assisted language learning (CALL) and LLM dialogue systems (Kim et al., 2019; Kohnke et al., 2023) strongly validates that low-stakes, judgement-free AI conversational practice reduces foreign language anxiety and promotes willingness to communicate. | **REQ-HYP-04 (Validated Requirement)**: Use structured, task-based AI conversational scenarios with explicit scaffolding and immediate corrective feedback as the primary speaking environment during A0–B1 stages to build productive fluency before live human interaction. |
