# Evidence Traceability Matrix: English Learning OS

> **Purpose**: This matrix serves as the single source of empirical truth for all pedagogical, psychological, and algorithmic decisions within the **English Learning OS**.
> All initial premises, common language learning beliefs, and working hypotheses are treated as **CANDIDATE CLAIMS / RESEARCH LEADS** until rigorously evaluated against empirical literature and conflicting evidence.
>
> Every material claim is classified under the project's canonical 8-tier epistemic hierarchy:
> 1. `ESTABLISHED`: Supported by robust, replicated meta-analyses, systematic reviews, or foundational cognitive consensus.
> 2. `STRONG EVIDENCE`: Supported by multiple peer-reviewed empirical studies / randomized trials with consistent outcomes.
> 3. `MODERATE EVIDENCE`: Supported by well-designed primary studies, but with boundary conditions or moderate sample sizes.
> 4. `LIMITED EVIDENCE`: Small-scale trials, preliminary laboratory data, or exploratory findings.
> 5. `EXPERT CONSENSUS`: Guidelines from authoritative international bodies (e.g., Council of Europe, Cambridge) or leading domain scholars without direct randomized trial evidence.
> 6. `PLAUSIBLE / HYPOTHESIS`: Conceptually sound theoretical model or hypothesis lacking direct empirical validation.
> 7. `UNSUPPORTED`: Claim lacking empirical evidence, based on myth, or contradicted by modern cognitive SLA research.
> 8. `CONFLICTING EVIDENCE`: Research literature shows divergent or contradictory results under different experimental conditions.

## 2. Adversarial Audit of Initial Product Hypotheses

| Hypothesis ID | Original Formulation | Audit Verdict | Empirical Rationale & Conflicting Findings | Transitioned Operational Requirement |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **HYP-01** | *Contextual sentence cards produce 2x higher retention than isolated word translation cards.* | **REVISED (Claim of 2x unsupported)** | Vocabulary literature (Webb, 2007; Waring, 2004) confirms sentence contexts aid syntax and collocation depth, but isolated word pairs allow faster initial receptive acquisition. A literal "2x retention multiplier" lacks empirical evidence. Reviewing sentence cards takes 2–3x longer per card. | **REQ-HYP-01 (Revised)**: Use lean bilingual cards (`CARD-01`) for initial form-meaning acquisition of the top 500 concrete lemmas; use contextual cloze cards (`CARD-02`) for polysemy, prepositions, collocations, and grammatical patterns. |
| **HYP-02** | *FSRS with an 85% retention target will minimize daily Anki review load compared to default Anki SM-2.* | **REVISED (Target parameter unvalidated)** | FSRS reduces reviews by ~20–30% vs SM-2, but 85% desired retention increases card lapses to 15% (vs 10% at 90%). Anki's CMRR shows that setting retention too low increases total study time due to high relearning overhead. For beginners, high lapse rates cause frustration and relearning churn. | **REQ-HYP-02 (Revised)**: Set initial desired retention to **90%** for foundational vocabulary (first 1,000 words) to guarantee high recognition stability. Dynamically evaluate downscaling to 85–87% only after accumulating $>500$ mature cards and reviews exceed 20 min/day. |
| **HYP-03** | *Prioritizing phonemic discrimination in weeks 1–4 will prevent fossilized pronunciation errors common among Portuguese speakers.* | **REVISED (Absolute prevention unsupported)** | High-Variability Phonetic Training (HVPT) significantly improves adult phonemic perception and production (Logan et al., 1991), but SLA literature (Han, 2004) confirms fossilization cannot be completely or permanently prevented by an initial 4-week drill block. | **REQ-HYP-03 (Revised)**: Implement dedicated contrastive phonological training targeting Brazilian Portuguese interference candidates (vowel contrasts, final epenthesis, rhythm) starting in Week 1, maintaining micro-drills integrated into daily listening/speaking. |
| **HYP-04** | *Controlled AI dialogue drills with real-time speech-to-text and grammar feedback will reduce anxiety compared to open-ended conversational apps.* | **SUPPORTED (With pedagogical boundaries)** | Empirical CALL research (Kim et al., 2019; Kohnke et al., 2023) validates that low-stakes, non-judgmental AI conversational practice significantly reduces foreign language anxiety and fosters willingness to communicate. | **REQ-HYP-04 (Validated Requirement)**: Use structured, task-based AI conversational scenarios with explicit scaffolding and immediate corrective feedback as the primary speaking environment during A0–B1 stages to build productive fluency before live human interaction. |
